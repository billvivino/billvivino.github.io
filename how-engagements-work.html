Clear Objectives, Visible Progress, and Decisions You Can Make

Begin with the problem, agree the next useful stage, and review the evidence before investing further. Software work can reveal new constraints; your engagement should still have clear responsibilities, outputs, and spending limits.

1. Define the first decision or deliverable

We agree what the initial work should establish or produce, what information and access are needed, and how the result will be reviewed.

2. Set the scope, cap, and review date

A focused review or implementation stage has an agreed scope and an hours or spending cap. Larger work can use weekly caps and milestone reviews. Hourly work is authorized in focused 5–10 hour blocks where appropriate, with a checkpoint before another block. A stage may span multiple blocks; its name does not imply a fixed fee or guaranteed completion within the first block.

3. Work directly with the people involved

Architecture, implementation, testing, demonstrations, stakeholder coordination, and documentation are part of the agreed paid work. We set a practical communication cadence and identify who can resolve product, data, and access decisions.

4. Make progress reviewable

At each checkpoint, review the agreed output, test evidence or findings, hours used, remaining risks, and recommended next step. If a dependency or discovery changes the plan, address it before expanding the work.

5. Continue, adjust, hand off, or stop

You control the next investment. A review may show that the original plan should change or that AI is not appropriate. Rollout, user enablement, and ongoing support are included only when explicitly scoped.

What is estimated and what is agreed?

The engagement agreement defines the initial outputs and review process. Completing an entire evolving product is a different commitment and cannot be guaranteed by an initial estimate. Any revised scope or additional spending requires a new agreement.
